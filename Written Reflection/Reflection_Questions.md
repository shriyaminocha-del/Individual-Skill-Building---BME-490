1. Choose one of the above skills that was unfamiliar or less familiar to you. What type of learning worked best for you to improve your abilities surrounding this skill? Why?

    I was most unfamiliar with the electronic component of this project---the soldering portion in particular! I've only soldered once before this, during a Project Tadpole meeting where I made a button to control a toy bear for children with impaired hand motor control. That was a much simpler project than the LED and photoresistor circuit. I learned how to stay safe and confident while soldering, the best technique to have a clean connection between wires, and how to use heat shrink wraps to keep the circuit from shorting and to clean up any exposed copper. This hands-on type of learning really helped me improve my abilities with soldering. I'd say the biggest hurdle was confidence: I was afraid I'd hurt myself or ruin circuit components by handling the (VERY HOT) soldering wand. But it turned out to be not as daunting as I thought it was, and I'm proud of the result!

2. How do you plan to use this improved skill in your team project?

    Our team project will require a signal processing physical mechanism. During our Thursday morning hospital shadowing, we learned that patients with Parkinson's struggle to stay in one place for too long, as it leads to joint stiffness. We originally planned to have the users of our speech therapy application place/hold their device at a set distance and do the exercises, but we realized holding their phones/tablets at the same distance for the whole duration could cause physical discomfort. 

    As such, we decided we might use some sort of physical sensor to record and send audio to our software interface. This way, patients can put the physical recorder at a set distance (like on a desk or bedside table), and move their arms/the device as much as they'd like. The physical sensor will likely require electronic components, hence soldering will be incredibly useful.
